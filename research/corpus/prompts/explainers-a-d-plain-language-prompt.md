# Research prompt: plain-language explainers for look-dev, lens and optics, framing and camera movement

Paste everything below the line into a research tool or give it to an agent. The answer is filed as a corpus card (`kind: owner-research`), checked against at least one authoritative source per term (a cinematography textbook, ASC or BSC material, or camera maker documentation), read-checked at or below grade 8.9, and dry-run by a fresh agent playing a 13-year-old (`docs/2-how-to/HOW-TO-INGEST-RESEARCH.md`). The reviewed set lives at `docs/3-reference/CAMERA-AND-LOOK-EXPLAINERS-A-D.md`.

---

You are writing help text for a free tool that 13-year-olds and adults with no film training use to plan short animated films. For each term in this list:

**[A] Look development (look-dev):** style frame, mood board, reference board, look bible, turnaround, color key, style lock, reference-only provenance, license note, approval status.

**[B] Optics / lens:** focal length (mm), 35 mm-equivalent focal length, angle of view / field of view (FOV), crop factor, aperture (f-stop), T-stop, focus distance, focal plane / plane of focus, depth of field (DOF), circle of confusion, hyperfocal distance, bokeh, tilt-shift, the Scheimpflug principle, miniature faking (synthetic tilt-shift blur: focus band position, focus band height, blur falloff), lens distortion (barrel, pincushion), projection (perspective vs orthographic / isometric).

**[C] Camera position and framing:** camera height, elevation / tilt angle, high angle, low angle, bird's-eye, top-down / overhead, azimuth, Dutch angle (roll), shot sizes (ECU, CU, MCU, MS, MWS / cowboy, WS / LS, EWS / ELS), establishing shot, insert, two-shot, over-the-shoulder (OTS), point of view (POV), headroom, lead room / nose room, rule of thirds, eyeline, 180-degree rule / screen direction.

**[D] Camera movement:** pan, tilt, dolly (push-in, pull-out), truck, pedestal, crane / jib, arc / orbit, zoom (optical vs digital), rack focus / focus pull, handheld vs locked-off, drone / aerial, parallax, move speed, easing (ease-in / ease-out).

Write: (1) a one-sentence plain explanation at a 6th–8th grade reading level, with no other jargon (if you must use another term, explain it in the same sentence); (2) "what it does to the picture": one sentence a beginner can picture; (3) "when to pick it": one sentence with an everyday example; (4) "if you're not sure": the safe default value and why; (5) a simple sketch description, i.e. what a tiny diagram or example frame should show, so we can draw it; (6) common beginner mistakes, max 2; (7) the source you relied on. Keep each field under 25 words. Never assume the reader knows any camera words.
